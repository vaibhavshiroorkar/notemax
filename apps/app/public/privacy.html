Notemax

Privacy

Last changed 22 October 2026

Notemax keeps your notes on your own device. Nothing leaves it unless you sign in and turn on sync.

Without an account

Your notes, settings and pictures are stored only in this browser or in the desktop app's folder on your computer. Nobody else can read them, including the people who make Notemax.

When you sign in

Signing in with Google or email creates an account with Supabase, the service Notemax uses for accounts. It stores your email address, your name and your profile picture, so you can sign in on other devices. Your notes are not stored there.

When you turn on sync

  • Notemax asks Google for access to its own files in your Google Drive (the drive.file permission). It can see only the files it creates in a folder named "Notemax". It cannot see any of your other files.
  • Your notes are encrypted on your device with a passphrase you choose, before they are sent. The files in Drive are unreadable without that passphrase. The passphrase never leaves your devices.
  • To refresh Google's access while you use the app, Notemax sends Google's sign-in token through a small Supabase function to Google. The function does not keep the token or anything else.

Google user data

Notemax uses the data it gets from Google only to sign you in and to sync your own notes between your own devices. It does not sell it, share it, use it for ads, or use it to train AI models. Notemax's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing your data

  • Turn off sync in Settings, then delete the "Notemax" folder in your Google Drive.
  • Remove Notemax's access at myaccount.google.com/connections.
  • To delete your account, open an issue on GitHub (below) and it will be removed.

Contact

Questions go to the issues page of the Notemax project on GitHub.
